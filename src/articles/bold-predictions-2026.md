I have a rule about hot takes on this site, and it is the same rule as everything else here: if it cannot be graded, it is not a take, it is a vibe. So these three are specific on purpose. Each one names two players or a finish, each one has a number I can point to, and each one has a grading rule written next to it so nobody (including me) gets to argue about what "right" meant in January.

They also go in the Receipts starting the first Monday of the season. Every week you will see where each one stands. That is going to be uncomfortable at least once. Good.

## 1. Jadarian Price Outscores Kenneth Walker III

**The call.** Price finishes 2026 with more half-PPR points than Walker.

**Why it is bold.** Walker is the reigning champion's running back, except now he is Kansas City's running back, going at pick 18 and sitting 12th on my own board. Price is the rookie who replaced him in Seattle, going at 57, sitting 41st. I am predicting the guy I rank 29 spots lower outscores the guy I rank higher. That is the definition of a bold call, and it is also a little bit of an admission that my board is hedged.

**Why I believe it anyway.** Walker played all 17 games last year: 221 carries for 1,027 yards and five touchdowns, 31 catches for 282 yards on 36 targets, 176.4 half-PPR points, 10.4 a game. That production happened in Seattle, in the offense that led the league in running back carries and red zone carries, and Seattle kept the offense and replaced the runner. Walker took the talent to Kansas City; the volume stayed home. I wrote the whole case in [the flag plant](/flag-plant/), so I will keep it short: 456 vacated backfield touches, a run game coordinator calling plays, a first-round pick starting Week 1. The vacated touches are the bet. A new zip code is the hedge.

**How it gets graded.** Total half-PPR points, Weeks 1 through 17. No games-played adjustment; if either one gets hurt, that is part of the call.

## 2. Brock Purdy Outscores Jayden Daniels

**The call.** Purdy averages more half-PPR points per game than Daniels in 2026.

**Why it is bold.** Daniels is going at pick 17, the fifth quarterback off the board. Purdy is going at 53, roughly the 14th quarterback. The market has decided one of these guys is a first-round fantasy asset and the other is a fifth-round afterthought. I have them a lot closer than that (Daniels 61st on my board, Purdy 85th, and the model has Purdy even lower at 107), and I still think the cheaper one wins.

**Why I believe it.** Start with what Purdy is when he plays. In 2025 he threw 284 passes for 2,167 yards, 20 touchdowns and 10 interceptions, ran for 147 yards and three scores, and averaged 19.7 half-PPR points a game, sixth among quarterbacks with at least six games. That followed 18.5 a game in 2023 (QB7) and 17.8 in 2024 (QB10). He played nine games last year because of a turf toe, and per-game he was still QB6, at 19.7 points a game. Career 8.6 yards per attempt in Shanahan's offense, with ten rushing touchdowns over the past three seasons, which is the sneaky part: he is not a runner, but he scores at the goal line. Ricky Pearsall is out for the year, which hurts. Evans, McCaffrey and Kittle are all back, which helps more. My note on the board says "value if healthy," and that is the whole sentence.

Now Daniels. He played seven games last year: 1,262 passing yards, eight touchdowns and three interceptions, 58 carries for 278 yards and two scores, 16.3 half-PPR points a game, QB17 among quarterbacks with at least six games. That is a long way from his rookie year, when he averaged 20.9 a game (QB6) across all 17. He is a phenomenal player, and the rushing floor is real. My hesitation is everything around him: a receiver room that got older rather than better (Diggs and McLaurin are both on the wrong side of 30), a run game I do not trust, and a new pass-heavy scheme that takes some of his rushing equity off the table by design.

**How it gets graded.** Half-PPR points per game, Weeks 1 through 17, minimum ten games for each. Per game rather than total because the whole argument is about who is better when he plays, not who stays upright.

## 3. Kenyon Sadiq Is This Year's Second-Half Tight End

**The call.** From Week 9 on, Sadiq is a top-8 tight end in half-PPR points per game, and he out-produces Mason Taylor over that stretch.

**Why it is bold.** He missed most of training camp and the entire preseason. Mason Taylor is the every-down tight end in New York to start the year, and the Jets have said as much. The rookie is going to open the season as a part-time player on a team that just spent a first-round pick on him a year after spending a second on Taylor, which almost nobody does.

**Why I believe it.** Two reasons, and the first is the player. Sixteenth overall pick out of Oregon, second-team All-American, led the FBS in touchdowns among tight ends last year with eight and set a school record for the position with 51 catches. Geno Smith has thrown to good tight ends before. Garrett Wilson is going to take the coverage.

The second reason is that we just watched this happen. Colston Loveland was a part-time player for half of his rookie year in Chicago, and then from Week 9 on he was a top-four tight end by any measure: ten games, 64 targets, 47 catches for 597 yards and six touchdowns, 11.9 half-PPR points a game, second only to Trey McBride in total points over those weeks and fourth per game (Bowers and Kittle were ahead of him in eight games each). Over the Bears' last four regular-season games he averaged eight targets a game, and 11.5 over the last two. Rookie tight ends do not arrive in September. They arrive in November, once the offense trusts them, and the ones who arrive tend to arrive all at once.

Oronde Gadsden is the other name people will bring up, and I will be honest about him: two enormous games in Weeks 7 and 8 (21 points a game) and then five a game the rest of the way. That is a flash, not a breakout. The Loveland version is the one I am predicting for Sadiq: not a week, a half.

**How it gets graded.** Half-PPR PPG among tight ends, Weeks 9 through 17, minimum six games. Top-8 is the bar, and he has to out-score Taylor per game over the same weeks. Both parts have to hit.
