Jahmyr Gibbs did not have his best statistical week. He had something more valuable: a role that finally stopped being shared. For most of last season Detroit split early downs almost evenly, and the box score never showed how little that arrangement mattered when the game got close.

What changed was not volume but composition. Gibbs took 78 percent of third downs and every snap inside the ten. Role Clarity moved from 54 to 81 — Workhorse territory, and the first time any Lions back has reached it.

> Volume tells you what happened. Composition tells you what the team believes.

## Why the box score missed it

Fourteen carries for 61 yards is a forgettable line. It is also what a full workload looks like in a game that was never close. The signal was not the yardage; it was that nobody else took a meaningful snap after the first quarter.

This is the gap the Role Clarity Score exists to fill. Opportunity share and snap counts both told you Gibbs was involved. Neither told you what kind of back Detroit thinks it has.

## Elsewhere on the board

The Rams quietly became a top-five neutral pass-rate offense, and Puka Nacua's Opportunity Trend is now the third-highest at the position. He moves up five.

CeeDee Lamb falls two, and it has nothing to do with him. His Scheme Fit dropped from 71 to 47 after the coordinator change pushed Dallas toward a shorter, YAC-driven passing game he was never built for. The talent is unchanged. The environment is not.
